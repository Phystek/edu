Why Diodes Pin the Voltage

ELEC2005 · Wk 2 · Diode Circuits

Both circuits below are a voltage source driving a series element and a load resistor Rload. On the left, the series element is an ordinary resistor R1 — its voltage divides with Rload in the usual way. On the right, the series element is a diode. Its curved, steep I–V characteristic forces the intersection with the load line into a narrow voltage band, almost regardless of VS or Rload. Move the sliders and watch how far each operating point travels.

The diode uses a standard exponential model (I = IS·(eV/nVT − 1), n = 1.8) for illustration — it is not any specific datasheet part, but the pinning behaviour it shows is general to real diodes.
