How a Solar Cell Makes Current

ELEC2005 · Week 11

A solar cell is a p–n junction diode with light shining on it. Watch the junction form and its electric field appear, photons free electron–hole pairs, the field pull them apart, and electrons flow round the circuit. By the end, the cell has turned into a current source in parallel with a diode, and the i–v curve has flipped into the generator convention used in the lecture.

A cartoon of the physics, with real numbers on the graph. Carriers are drawn far fewer, far larger and far slower than real ones, and their random motion is stylised. The graph and readouts use the ideal one-cell model from the lecture (Isc = 3.4 A at 1000 W/m², I0 = 6×10−10 A, 25 °C).
