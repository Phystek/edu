A BJT's IC–VBE characteristic shifts left as it heats up — the same current flows at a lower VBE (that's the 2 mV/°C you've seen). What decides whether that shift causes trouble is how the transistor is biased. A fixed VBE (direct bias, no RE) is a vertical line on this plot: as the curve slides left underneath it, the intersection races up — runaway. A sloped RE bias line pins the operating point down, exactly like a load line constrains a Q-point. Drag the sliders and watch where the curve crosses each line.

Simplified model: this widget derives the shifting IC–VBE curve directly from the lecture's 2 mV/°C VBE temperature coefficient. It treats ΔT as an externally imposed junction temperature rise rather than a full power-dissipation↔temperature feedback loop, holds thermal voltage VT constant, and assumes IE≈IC (base current neglected in the emitter KVL). This isolates the electrical stabilisation mechanism for teaching purposes — real thermal runaway also involves self-heating and finite β.
