ELEC2005 · Week 10

As the current in a coil rises, the core's operating point moves up its B–H curve. At first the curve is a straight line and its slope gives the inductance you would calculate from n, Ac, ℓm and μ. Once the core starts to saturate the slope falls away, and the inductance falls with it. Raise the current and watch both plots.

Simplified curve. This tool uses a smooth, idealised B–H curve with no hysteresis, on a fixed core (ℓm = 150 mm, Ac = 4 cm²). A real core has a sharper knee and traces a loop rather than a single line. The inductance shown is the local slope: what a small change in current would see at that operating point.
