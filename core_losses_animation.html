Core Losses: Hysteresis and Eddy Currents

ELEC2005 · Week 10

Switch a transformer on with nothing connected to it and its core still gets warm. Two things waste power inside the iron every cycle: the domains being dragged back and forth (hysteresis), and currents swirling round inside the iron itself (eddy currents). Watch both happen, see what makes each one worse, and why both end up as one resistor in the equivalent circuit.

Relative numbers, exaggerated sizes. Losses are shown relative to a reference core (soft steel, 50 Hz, 1 T peak, 10 laminations), using how each loss scales: hysteresis loss follows f × the area of the drawn loop, and eddy loss follows f²B²t². The animation runs about 150 times slower than 50 Hz, the loop is wider than real transformer steel, and the laminations are drawn far thicker than the 0.23 to 0.35 mm of a real core.
