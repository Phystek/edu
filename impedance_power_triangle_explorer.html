Impedance · Complex Power

Build up an impedance Z = R + jX with the sliders below. Your lecture slides introduced Z algebraically, without drawing it as a triangle — the impedance triangle here is a visual tool being introduced for the first time, not something you've already seen on a slide. It's worth showing because it's the exact same shape as the power triangle you have covered, sharing the same angle φ.

The phasor diagram on the left is different in kind from the other two, not just in appearance: V̄ and Ī are real oscillating quantities — actual snapshots of things rotating in the circuit, the same idea from the phasor explorer. Z and S̄ are not oscillating phasors of anything — Z is a fixed ratio V̄/Ī, and S̄ is a power quantity computed from V̄ and Ī. All three happen to share the same angle φ, which is exactly why they're drawn side by side here — but only the left-hand picture is a phasor diagram in the strict sense.
